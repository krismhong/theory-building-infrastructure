# Level 5: Clause

**Your unit** is the clause and attribution. **Your question:** are reported claims and the author's steps distinguishable and consistent with context?

**Read** only assigned sentences/parts, all cited clauses there, or every sentence in full mode. Check source fidelity only against supplied records, never memory/web. Skeletons are navigation, not substitutes for missing full text.

## Checks

**5.1 Whose claim? (up)** Distinguish reports from deductions/applications. Decision: "Is X what Author reports, or your inference?"

**5.2 Source fidelity (down).** Check strength/scope against supplied source records. Missing/incomplete records make fidelity indeterminate. Decision: "The record reports X in S; what supports the broader attribution?"

**5.3 Inferential attribution (down).** Distinguish a source's step from the author's use of its premises. Citation placement/clause order are conventions. Decision: "Does Author state this conclusion, or is it your inference?"

**5.4 Attributed scope (across).** Make clear which claims a citation supports. It can support several clauses if the record does. Missing records cannot prove mismatch. Decision: "Which claims are attributed versus deduced?"

**5.5 Inference matches premises (down).** A step to another population, analysis level, or stronger conclusion needs connecting reasoning. Decision: "What premise extends teams to firms?"

**5.6 Consistent evidence role (down).** Do not represent a record as establishing more than it reports. Do not rank best sources, impose a source count, or judge evidence sufficiency. Decision: "Does the record establish X, or is X an assumption?"

**5.7 Identifiable claim (down).** Flag materially different interpretations that change reasoning, not technical language or plain-word preferences. Missing definitions can make judgment indeterminate. Decision: "Does this clause claim A or B?"

**5.8 Reference integrity (whole draft).** Citation/reference discrepancies in supported author-year formats are `reference_check`, separate from validity. Regex coverage is limited and can misread formats; do not duplicate it when the task says it ran. Decision: "Add the entry, or correct this citation?"

No matching record proves that the source/evidence is true. Clean logic establishes neither empirical truth nor evidence sufficiency.
