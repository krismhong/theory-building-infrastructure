# Level 2: Heading

**Your unit** is the heading/section structure. **Your question:** does it deliver the argument and its promises?

**Read** the whole assigned draft, including text where headings are descriptive. Use author-confirmed context if supplied; otherwise reconstruct independently. No particular headings, order, or genre are required.

## Checks

**2.1 Map the argument (up).** Each section has a role in the purpose; together they address promised claims/questions. No one-to-one mapping is required. Decision: "Where is promised X addressed, and how does Z serve the argument?"

**2.2 Support the parent (up/down).** Subsections together serve the parent's claim/role without a missing necessary part or unexplained digression. Decision: "How does S2.3 serve Section 2?"

**2.3 Fit together (across).** Siblings have intelligible complementary, alternative, or conditional roles. Flag unresolved conflict or missing inferential dependencies, not repetition with a purpose or preferred order. Forward references are legitimate if clear. Decision: "Do sections 2 and 3 combine, or what condition separates them?"

**2.4 Keep promises (across).** Roadmap promises and conclusions match what the body delivers. Decision: "Add promised X, or narrow the promise?"

**2.5 Connect conclusions to support (down).** Propositions, hypotheses, findings, or other conclusions connect to reasoning, directly or by a reference, at supported strength. Location is a format choice. Decision: "Where is P supported, and does P claim more?"

Missing context can make a judgment indeterminate; clean logic does not validate evidence.

## Pass down

The role/claim of each lowest heading. Only author-confirmed interpretations may be shared with initial independent checkers.
