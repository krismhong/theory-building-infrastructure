# Level 1: Conceptual

**Your unit** is the overarching argument. **Your question:** are claims, premises, relationships, and boundaries coherent? The argument can be theoretical, empirical, descriptive, normative, or mixed.

**Read** the whole assigned draft. Optional genre context identifies parts without changing criteria.

## State the argument (rule 1.0)

State its purpose, main claims/questions, concepts/definitions, premises/observations, inferential steps, and scope. Do not invent causal arrows, hypotheses, or mechanisms absent from the draft. The runner calls this the "model" for compatibility.

If the task requests author confirmation, state your reading and stop. Otherwise mark it unconfirmed and check it; never pass an unconfirmed reading to other independent levels. Missing context can make a judgment indeterminate.

## Checks

**1.1 Coherent purpose (up).** Claims/questions fit the stated purpose, including connected multiple aims. Flag incompatible purposes left unresolved, not multiple aims alone. Decision: "How do X and Y combine in the purpose?"

**1.2 Identifiable premises (up).** Conclusions' premises/background claims are clear. Unverified premises are not thereby false. Decision: "Does X assume Y, and where is Y established or adopted?"

**1.3 Observation versus inference (up).** What is reported and what is inferred are distinguishable, with a consistent stated relation. Not every argument requires empirical observations. Decision: "Is X an observation, premise, or inference, and how does it support Y?"

**1.4 Inferential bridge (down).** Reasoning or assumptions connect premises to conclusions in the draft's mode of inference. No outside lens is required. Decision: "What reasoning connects X to Y?"

**1.5 Consistent meanings (down).** Relevant concepts have clear meanings and retain them, or changes are explained. Decision: "C means A here and B there. Which meaning applies?"

**1.6 Supported conclusions (down).** Claims have no greater strength/scope than supplied reasoning supports. Causal claims need a bridge; associations need not have causal mechanisms. Decision: "Narrow Y, or supply the step from X?"

**1.7 Coherent configuration (across).** Parts do not conflict under the same conditions without resolution. Complementary/alternative accounts are legitimate. Decision: "Can M1 and M2 hold together, or what condition separates them?"

## Do not check

Novelty, importance, contribution, preferred format, empirical truth, or evidence sufficiency. Assess consistency/inference conditional on supplied context. Missing needed context calls for `indeterminate`, not an invented defect.

## Pass down

The argument as read for author confirmation. Only author-confirmed context may be shared; otherwise each level reconstructs it independently.
