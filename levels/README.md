# The levels

The table moves from the whole argument to smaller parts of its text. A reviewer can start here to see what each separate reading is meant to examine.

| Level | Unit | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Overall argument | Are claims, premises, relationships, and boundaries coherent? |
| 2 | Headings/sections | Do they deliver the argument and promises? |
| 3 | Paragraph points | Do sibling points make the parent's case? |
| 4 | Sentences | Do they support the paragraph's point? |
| 5 | Clauses/attribution | Are reports and inferential steps clear and consistent? |

For each unit, the checker asks what it contributes to the larger argument, whether its smaller parts support it, and whether it fits with other units at the same depth. For example, several paragraph points may jointly support a section: the section is their "parent," and the paragraphs are "siblings." Those points may complement one another, offer alternatives, or apply under different conditions. Their order alone does not establish that they fit logically.

The criteria assess arguments generally. Optional genre context adds no criteria. Style, novelty, truth, and evidence sufficiency are separate; missing context can leave indeterminate findings.

Initial checkers work independently; only context confirmed by the author may be shared. Full mode covers all assigned paragraphs and clauses. Gated mode uses fewer tasks, with no established accuracy advantage. A later, separate verifier compares findings across reports. The optional conferring protocol is a different process, described in the execution guide.

Lists of first sentences help locate material; they do not require every paragraph to begin with its main point. Topic-sentence-first remains default structure guidance, with placement problems distinguished from missing, inconsistent, or unsupported claims. Automated text-pattern and word-choice checks remain separate from argument judgments.
