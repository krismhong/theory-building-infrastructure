# Level 4: Sentence

**Your unit** is the sentence. **Your question:** do sentences support their paragraph's point?

**Read** only assigned paragraphs, in full. Full mode assigns all independently; gated mode supplies selected paragraphs/reasons. Identify the point from full text, not necessarily the opening. Locations use the supplied heading, heading-local paragraph number, then sentence number.

## Checks

**4.1 Serve the point (up).** Sentences develop, support, qualify, or provide needed context. A new idea is not automatically a digression. Decision: "How does sentence 4's Y serve X?"

**4.2 Support the point (up).** Together they support its stated strength/scope. Decision: "The point claims X for all firms, but this reasoning covers large firms. Narrow it, or supply the step?"

**4.3 Fit together (across).** Sentences do not contradict under the same conditions or leave necessary inferential steps missing. Qualifications, alternatives, examples, purposeful repetition, and forward references are legitimate. Decision: "Sentence 4 limits X while sentence 2 generalizes it. Which conditions apply?"

**4.4 Match support (down).** Quantifiers, scope, and causal language match supplied reasoning. Association alone does not derive causation; missing design assumptions can leave judgment indeterminate. Decision: "What causal premise connects this association to the conclusion?"

**4.5 Consistent claim type (down).** Descriptive, normative, and mixed arguments are legitimate. Flag a shift from what happens to what ought to happen without a connecting premise. "Should" alone is not a defect. Decision: "Which normative premise supports this recommendation?"

**4.6 Identifiable actor/action (down).** Materially different readings about who does what must be resolved. Passive voice/phrasing alone is not a defect. Decision: "Who acts here, and does that change the claim?"

Judge consistency/inference in context, not empirical truth or evidence sufficiency. Missing needed context calls for indeterminate.

## Pass down

Sentences needing closer clause checks as "<paragraph's first sentence, verbatim> | <sentence, verbatim> | <reason>". The opening is a location anchor. Level 5 also checks assigned cited clauses.
