# Empirical paper guidance (optional context)

Use with `--genre genres/empirical-paper.md` when chosen by the author. This helps interpret an empirical argument; it adds no logical-defect criteria.

- Identify questions, claims/hypotheses where present, premises, and scope. Deductive, inductive, exploratory, qualitative, quantitative, and mixed designs are legitimate. Hypotheses are not required.
- Trace rationale, design, observations/measures, analysis, findings, and interpretation where present. No fixed headings or section order are required.
- Distinguish predictions, observations, estimates, and interpretations. Association alone does not derive causation; a causal argument may depend on design assumptions absent from assigned context. Missing needed context requires `indeterminate`, not declaring a conclusion false.
- Track scope, uncertainty, units, attribution, and inferences. Data summaries/source records are argument context, not independently verified evidence.

Checkers never rewrite Methods/Results, run analyses, audit raw data, or validate measurement, identification, reproducibility, or evidence sufficiency. Those require separate evidence and expertise. Consistent arguments can have false premises or inadequate evidence. A clean report is not an empirical-validity finding.
