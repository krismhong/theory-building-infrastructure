# Slack Time and Learning in Project Teams

*A short fictional draft for trying the checker. Its argument, its cases, and its sources are invented.*

## Introduction

Research on project teams holds that deadlines focus attention and so improve learning (Avery, 2014; Brandt & Cole, 2017). On this view, a team that works close to its deadline examines its mistakes more carefully, because pressure concentrates effort on the task at hand.

However, teams in three software firms learned least from the projects they finished closest to their deadlines (Dorsey, 2019). In each firm, the teams that finished early held longer reviews and changed more of their practices afterward.

We argue that slack time, the time a team has left between finishing its work and its deadline, is what allows a team to learn from a project. Teams without slack move to the next task before they reflect, so their mistakes go unexamined.

## Slack Time as the Condition for Reflection

Slack time is the interval between the completion of a project's work and its deadline. It is distinct from total project duration, because a long project can still end with no slack.

Slack gives a team time that no task claims, and reflection needs such time. Teams that finished early held longer reviews (Dorsey, 2019), which this mechanism would explain: with slack, members can review what went wrong without taking time from the next project.

Teams that protect slack time would perform better on later projects, because reflection can improve later performance (Evers, 2016).

**Proposition 1:** The more slack time a team has at the end of a project, the more the team learns from that project.
